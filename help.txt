Viscera ranking:

In Items, choose "Import from: Viscera". The list defaults to sorting by Combined DPS.
Use the sort dropdown for Full DPS, minion-only stats, defensive stats, or Name.
Each candidate uses midpoint modifier rolls, with normal game rounding; this is not an average of DPS across all rolls.
Ranking replaces the selected compatible slot, or uses the best compatible slot with "Any slot".
Other equipment is kept in place. Tooltips and imported candidates use the same midpoint rolls.
Candidates without an available compatible slot are marked unavailable. Rankings follow the active skill and Configuration.
This is a stat comparison, not a recommendation that an item improves overall boss survivability.

Measured stat weights:

In Calcs, click "Stat Weights...", select a damage profile and DPS metric, then Calculate.
The sortable report measures DPS, PoB Total EHP, and mean simulated survival time by adding one stat at a time.
Profiles are isolated comparisons: your active Configuration, equipment, and passive allocation are unchanged.
Steps use average high-tier gaps on the smallest eligible gear, rather than arbitrary amounts or whole affix rolls.
Fractional increments are intentional averages. Stats without a comparable tier step are marked unavailable.
The percentage and optional absolute gains apply to one average tier step added to your current build.
These are reference sensitivities, not actual upgrades to your equipped affixes or equal-cost comparisons.
Global flat defences are not local armour/evasion/ES rolls on a base. Caps, rounding, and breakpoints can produce zero weights.
Survival uses the same recovery-aware simulation as the optimizer, with a 30-second ceiling, not a real boss rotation.
N/A indicates an undefined percentage, missing output, or non-finite metric. Copy Report copies the displayed units.

Optimize Tree:

Use "Optimize Tree" at the bottom of the Tree tab after loading a character.
If no skill groups are included in Full DPS, that metric uses the selected skill's Combined DPS plus its minion.
Choose what to improve and a damage profile, then press "Find a better tree".
The default goal is "Damage AND survival - improve both". Survival means simulated mean time alive.
No goal may reduce mean time alive below its starting value. "Survive longer" allows at most 0.5% starting-DPS loss.
The Overview shows build-aware safety defaults and four defensive questions, without a wall of sliders.
Advanced controls expose the damage metric, swap size and optional safety overrides.
Full DPS measures skills included in Full DPS; selected skill measures the current skill/minion.
Profiles include the current or another saved Configuration, generic Pinnacle, native normal-pinnacle skill
presets at maximum roll, and a synthetic mixed-boss pressure test. Maven Memory Game is excluded from built-in profiles.
The mixed profile is an approximation, not calibrated Feared encounter data or a full boss rotation.
Changing the search profile, searching and applying a tree never change your active Configuration.
Built-in profiles preserve other player conditions and custom modifiers, replacing only incoming-hit settings
and the managed synthetic background-DoT block. Saved Configurations use all their own conditions.
Create/edit reusable profiles in the Configuration tab. Profile selection is remembered for this session.
Balanced requires higher DPS and longer mean time alive than the starting tree, ranked by their relative product.
If time alive ties, including at the 30-second ceiling, buffer margin must improve instead.
"Survive longer" maximizes mean time alive, using buffer margin only for equal times.
"More damage" maximizes DPS without reducing starting mean time alive; ties favor time alive, then buffer margin.
These objectives do not change ordinary PoB EHP and are not boss-kill or encounter-readiness predictions.
The four-question readout shows maximum-hit and recovery snapshots, and explicitly flags missing tests:
there is no boss-specific hit certification, two-hit recovery test, timed degen escape or guard/flask-off audit.
It runs a 30-second benchmark using configured enemy damage, attack/cast time and mitigation.
Mean time alive is the survival objective, capped at 30 seconds. Buffer margin is the previous resource-weighted
score: expected time alive weighted by remaining usable Life/Mana/ES buffer. It is not EHP or literal survival time.
Recovery includes calculated player regeneration, capped/ramping leech and on-hit gains from the selected
skill and enabled triggers, recoup, recharge, and block/suppression recovery. Mine/proxy leech is not
assumed to heal the player. Recurring skill spending, temporary mine reservation and build degeneration
are included, along with native averaged enemy-ailment degeneration. Enemy DoT uses DoT conversion/mitigation
and bypasses hit-only buffers and loss prevention.
Complete hit outcomes are branched for avoidance, block and suppression; lethal hits cannot be healed
after death. Probability states are deterministically compressed to 16 nearby resource states.
This is an estimate: attack/spell Average mode is a mixed profile; avoidance rolls are independent.
Configured buffs and offensive rates remain active. Guard, aegis and ally buffers are initial snapshots,
not timed recasts/resummons. Flask availability, on-kill healing, damage bursts from multiple enemies,
dynamic leech conversions and changes to offensive cadence during combat are not scheduled.
Energy shield recharge is conservatively interrupted by damage/spending; special uninterrupted-recharge
effects are not simulated. Strong builds can tie at the 30-second ceiling; this is not infinite survival.
The search exchanges connected branches up to the selected swap size, using the same allocated point count.
Mastery allocations and selected effects are optimized, without selecting duplicate effects.
Ascendancy passives can be reallocated within the selected primary and secondary ascendancies.
Ascendancy selections, runegrafts and occupied jewel sockets are never changed.
Normal and ascendancy points keep separate fixed budgets; neither can be spent as the other.
The primary and secondary ascendancies share the existing total ascendancy-point budget.
Ascendancy start nodes remain fixed. Zero-cost options (including Reliquarian relic choices)
can change within the selected ascendancy; lock a specific option to preserve it.
Swap size counts spent points, not zero-cost options on the branch.
Passive-point grants cannot be lost if needed to cover the fixed normal-point budget.
Empty jewel sockets can be allocated or refunded; socketed jewels are never changed.
Passives on cluster jewels in allocated sockets can be reallocated.
Required-node locks are saved with each tree and also protect nodes needed to keep them connected.
Node blocks are saved with each tree and exclude both the blocked node and routes through it.
Blocking an allocated node requires the optimizer to refund it before a result can be used.
Mastery locks preserve both the allocation and its selected effect. Requiring a node clears its block;
blocking a node clears its requirement. Both markings support undo/redo.
Stop keeps the best result found so far. "Use Best as New Tree" preserves the original tree.
New optimized tree names include a local-time timestamp in yyyymmddhhmmss format.
Close (or Escape) discards the search. No changes are made unless you use the result.
This is a local search, not an exhaustive/global optimizer; it can miss improvements requiring other swaps.
Results must leave non-negative unreserved mana, positive unreserved life, and meet item/gem attribute
requirements (including Omniscience). These checks cannot be disabled.
Build-aware defaults protect elemental/chaos resistances, casting resources and large-hit protection.
Chaos resistance is unlocked for a 1-Life, chaos-immune build. Block is locked only at 50%+ attack/spell
block or with recovery on block; suppression at 50%+; ailment avoidance if any elemental ailment is fully avoided.
Unlocked defenses still affect simulated survival; only their separate "preserve this stat" floor is off.
Advanced controls can enable or disable each optional floor and reset to build-aware defaults.
Enabled allowable-reduction sliders start at 0% and range to 100% for elemental/chaos resistance, attack AND spell
block, spell suppression, elemental ailment avoidance, and casting resources (mana).
0% permits a fixed 0.5% reduction tolerance; 100% removes that optional requirement.
Reductions are relative to each starting value, not percentage points or the last accepted tree.
Negative resistance can fall by the selected percentage of its absolute starting value.
Grouped stats are checked separately. These preserve effective values, not overcap, and do not force
uncapped defences to reach their caps. At 0%, complete suppression and elemental ailment avoidance stay complete.
Explicit safety choices are remembered after starting a search while the tree tab is open.
Casting headroom checks separate burst-capacity and sustained-recovery ratios, not just pool size.
Burst demand includes one use of each recurring skill plus one movement, one guard and the most
expensive other on-demand skill. Repeated demand includes the selected skill at full calculated
cadence, enabled triggered skills, and ongoing skill drains. Guaranteed every-second-use refunds
reduce sustained demand, not the upfront burst budget.
Automated Pyroclast includes peak first-detonation reservation from unrestricted throwing samples,
including extra-use/re-arm timing. It restores the selected skill's static mine-reservation snapshot.
0% preserves each starting ratio within 0.5%, but never below 1 (the complete modeled budget).
An unsafe starting tree must improve to feasibility; it is not silently approved as the baseline.
Positive allowable reductions can relax that budget, and 100% disables the headroom requirement.
There is no simulated protected reserve or early stop to throwing. Sustained recovery must fund
full-cadence spending indefinitely, so a large initial pool cannot hide a recurring deficit.
This is a conservative budget estimate using configured costs/rates and regeneration, not an
exact action scheduler. Manual utility use frequency is unknown; repeated utility spam, flask
timing, hit-based recovery/drains, random trigger clustering and Life/ES payment are not guaranteed.
Energy Shield payment and unsupported mine-reservation timing are explicitly reported as unchecked
models that require disabling the casting-headroom constraint to optimize.
"Preserve large-hit protection" keeps every damage type's maximum hit at least at its starting value.
If the starting tree fails a requirement, the search can still find a feasible branch swap; no invalid
tree can be applied. Larger swaps may help, but this local search cannot guarantee a feasible result.
Results depend on your skills, gear and enemy configuration. Review other defensive trade-offs.

---[Timeless Jewels]

DEV[Developer Information:]
DEV[The Timeless jewels determine what effect they have on a node based on the "Look up Tables" in \src\Data\TimelessJewelData]
DEV[The LuTs for the timeless jewels come from https://github.com/Regisle/TimelessJewelData]
DEV[ ]
DEV[These can be generated if that repo is out of date (see RELEASE.md)]
DEV[ ]
Timeless jewels modify nodes in a radius based on their seed, the same seed will apply the same changes to the same small/notable nodes so that is the number you look for. The Conqueror (name on the jewel) only affects the keystone.

Path of Building has an inbuilt tool in the tree tab to search through seeds to find stats that would be good for your build.
Clicking the "Find Timeless Jewel" button on the bottom bar in the tree tab will open a UI that lets you:
Select which jewel type, which jewel socket to search for, and which conqueror from that jewel
    ("Any" will just use the first conqueror if you add it to build, more on that later)

The filter nodes option makes it only check nodes that are allocated when searching a jewel socket.
Next, a node change/addition can be selected with the "search for node" dropdown,
    The values for the stats added are controlled by primary/secondary (in the case of 2 stats from 1 node) node weights
    If that type has already been added, then moving those sliders will change the values
    These values can also be edited directly in the "Desired Nodes" box
    A minimum weight can be added for a node. This will filter results to only seeds with at least that much weight from those nodes.
    If the minimum weight bar is at max, it will turn it into "required", this just means the node needs to show up at least once

There are 2 different boxes these weights can be entered into, a basic one, and the "fallback" weights.
    If a node is in both it will only use the value from desired. The fallback weights can automatically be populated by using
    the fallback weight mode, selecting a method of determining the weights (a stat to sort by) and clicking generate.
    These can then be copied from the fallback nodes box to the desired nodes box if you want to generate a different type
        eg. If you want to sort by combined dps and ehp, you generate one, copy it over and then generate the other

Lastly is the search results box. This will list seeds from the best to the worst based on your search parameters.
    Double-clicking one will add the jewel with that seed and keystone to your build.
    Clicking "Copy Trade URL" will copy a few results to a trade link which can be pasted into your browser.
    Doing this multiple times will copy later results in case the first one didn't show results.
    If you click 1 result and shift-click another it will highlight multiple, you can then click "Copy trade URL" to search all of them

Militant Faith jewels also have the option to select the other mods on the jewel

Advanced tricks:
    For Militant Faith, if you don't want any nodes to change, you can just sort by devotion. As unchanged nodes add devotion, the ones with the most devotion will be the ones with unchanged nodes

---[Loadouts]

Loadouts can be selected from the dropdown in the top middle of the screen. Selecting a Loadout will load all four sets at once. These are automatically registered based on one of three conditions:

    1) All four sets share the same name and colour formatting, e.g. "Leveling"
        - If you have a set named ^4Leveling^7, it will not match to other sets named Leveling

    2) All four sets have the same alphanumeric identifier inside of braces { } at the end of the name, e.g. "Leveling Tree {1}", "Leveling Items {1}", "Leveling Skills {1}", "Leveling Config {1}"
        - If you would like a single set to be used in multiple Loadouts, you can put the identifiers in the braces separated by commas. For example, an Item Set could be named "Early Game {1,2}" and there could be Tree, Skill, and Config Sets with {1} and {2}, resulting in two loadouts linked to the same Item Set
        - The name of the Loadout in the dropdown is based on the name of the Tree Set, identifiers are shown for clarity when using sets multiple times
        - These sets can have differing colour formatting so long as the identifier texts match

    3) If there is only one set for a set type (except passive tree), e.g. "Default" config set, it will be assigned to all existing loadouts.

The "New Loadout" option allows the user to create all four sets from a single popup for convenience.
The "Sync" option is a backup option to force the UI to update in case the user has changed this data behind the scenes.


---[Party Tab]

The Party tab Allows you to import support characters and have their auras and curses apply

To import a build it must be exported with "Export support" enabled in the import/export tab and must be imported in the party tab
You can import a specific type like aura or curse, or import to all
You can also set it to append to a section rather than replace it (curses and links are always replaced if new one has a curse/link)

This does not add the auras, curses or links into the skills tab, but they do show up on calcs tab under "aura and buff skills" as well as "curses and debuffs" respectively
They also show other effects they add, like when physical damage reduction is applied by an aurabot with the Guardian node

Auras with the highest effect will take priority
Your curses will take priority over a support's

Links skills use stats from party members if they are exported
Some Enemy conditions and Modifiers may not be properly exported, please let us know if something is broken
Some auras like Mines which use a stack value for their effect will not apply as their stack value is missing

---[Skills Tab]

The skills tab allows you to plan out the skills you will use for your character. To access this tab, click on "Skills" in the tab at the top left of the screen next to "Tree" and "Items".

From here, you can view all of the skills in your build. There are a few different UI elements that you can use to group your skills and make DPS calculations easier.

The top left window lets you view Socket Groups. Skills can be organized into Socket Groups to show which support gems are applied to certain skills. You can add a socket group by clicking New and selecting a skill from the drop-down.
For example, you could add a socket group for Vitality and then attach Arrogance to the group as a support.
You can also add labels and organize your skills by type (auras, mobility, damage, etc). This is a great way to keep track of skill changes across skill sets as you level.

Skill sets can be thought of as collections of socket groups. There is a select box above the socket groups window that will let you switch between them.
A great way to use these is to create skill sets for different level gaps, allowing you to plan out which skills you can run at certain points in your levelling progression.
They are also a nice way to showcase different skill options for your build. One set could show the skills you run to maximize DPS, while another could show a better setup for survivability or maybe mapping. Click Manage next to the select box to add, remove, rename, and copy skill sets.

If you click on a skill in one of your socket groups, a table will appear on the right showing you the details of that group. You can set which piece of gear the skills are socketed in, as well as the level of each skill, the variant, quality, and count.
Hovering over a skill will display a full description of that skill and toggling the "Enabled" checkbox will enable or disable that skill from your build. You can also use the "Include in Full DPS" checkbox to include or exclude that skill from the Full DPS calculation displayed on the left panel.
This can be useful for seeing how much damage you are getting from specific skills as well as specific groups of skills in your gear. You may also want to make a socket group simply for display purposes and then you can exclude that from DPS calculations.

Finally, underneath the Socket Groups window is the Gem Options box. This will let you configure the level of your skills gems as well as sort them by different types of DPS. Really useful for viewing total potential damage vs damage at different levels.


---[Items Tab]

The Item Tab in Path of Building allows you to plan out the gear you will use for your character. To access the Item Tab, click on the "Items" tab located at the top of the screen.

From here, you can view all of the gear slots for your character, such as your weapon, chest armour, and boots. To add an item to a slot, click on the arrow button on the right side of the slot. This will bring up a list of all the items available in your build, which are visible in the "All items" window.
If you wish to add more items, you can copy and paste items using CTRL+C (from the game or trade website) and CTRL+V into the "Shared items" window. Additionally, you can craft items using the "Craft item..." button on the top or create completely customizable items using the "Create custom..." option.

The item sets section located at the top of the screen can be used to save multiple sets of items and easily switch between them. You can add, remove, rename, and copy new sets using the "Manage..." button.

If you want to search for item upgrades, you can use the "Trade for these items" button to quickly search for items that can improve your gear.

---[Similar Builds Popup]

The Similar Builds Popup allows you to search for builds similar to your current character.

To access the Similar Builds Popup, click on the "Similar Builds" button located at the top of the screen.

It will fetch the builds most similar to your character and sort them by the latest league and match score.

For best results, make sure to select your main item set, tree, and skills before opening the popup.
If you are using leveling gear/tree, it will match with other leveling builds.
